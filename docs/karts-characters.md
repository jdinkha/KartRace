# Karts, physics, controls and characters

Read before changing kart models, kart physics, controls, bumping, mascots/characters or grandstand fans.

## Karts, wheels and the garage
- A kart's looks are two separate items: the **kart** (the body) and the **wheels**. Any kart works with any wheels. They're listed in the `Garage` ModuleScript (ReplicatedStorage): `KARTS` (each with where its front and rear wheels go) and `WHEELS` (each with its front and rear size in studs across).
- The looks are template Models under the module: `Garage.Karts.<id>` and `Garage.Wheels.<id>`. Kart templates are centred on the hitbox centre with the front at -Z (the road is 1.1 studs below). Wheel templates are 2 studs across, centred on the axle, which runs along X. Parts with a `Paint` attribute take the racer's colour. `Garage.build(kartId, wheelsId, color)` clones and scales them into one model.
- Current items: karts `Classic` (the original: rounded nose and sides, racing stripe, headlights, spoiler, twin exhausts) and `F1` ("Formula", made in Blender: nose + front wing, sidepods, airbox, rear wing). Wheels `Classic` ("Chunky", hubcaps in the kart's colour) and `Slick` (Blender: slick tyre with a yellow stripe and a painted rim). Everything is free and unlocked for everyone.
- `buildKart(color, cframe, kartId, wheelsId)` makes the invisible hitbox, seat and attachments, then welds on the `Garage.build` looks. The looks never collide; every combo has the same hitbox. Keep the hitbox and seat where they are when adding karts.
- **Stats:** how a combo drives comes from `stats` on each entry in `KARTS` and `WHEELS` (edit them there; the list and what each does is `Garage.STATS`, explained at the top of the module): `speed` (top speed, boosts too), `acceleration`, `weight` (bumps: hit a kart twice as heavy and you bounce off twice as far, it half as far), `handling` (turn rate, so a tighter turning circle; bots also take corners faster), `offroad` (off-road top speed, capped at road speed) and `miniTurbo` (how fast a drift charges its blue and orange mini-turbos). Each is a multiplier, 1 = the Classic kart on Chunky wheels; a racer's stat = kart's × wheels' (`Garage.stats`). `buildKart` puts them on the kart model as `Stat_speed`, `Stat_acceleration`... attributes, and `kartStat` reads them: in `driveStep`/`bumpKarts` on the client, and in `driveBot` for CPUs (they use their random kart's stats, except `miniTurbo`: they don't drift). Right now every kart and wheel set is all 1s, so nothing drives differently yet. Rarer or paid combos are meant to be better later; the garage menu doesn't show stats yet.
- Who drives what: players get their `Kart`/`Wheels`/`Character` attributes. CPU racers get a random kart and random wheels. The lobby showroom shows three combos (`showKarts` in `buildLobby`).
- Only one menu is open at a time: opening the garage closes the RACE track menu (`RaceHUD.TrackMenu`, RaceClient's) and opening RACE closes the garage (`Garage.Panel`). Each script closes the other's in the same frame it opens its own, so they swap with no flicker.
- The garage menu (`GarageClient`): a GARAGE button on the left (hidden while racing) opens a panel with a turning 3D preview of your combo (with your character in it) and rows of character, kart and wheel cards (ViewportFrames). Each row's canvas is sized from its `UIListLayout.AbsoluteContentSize` (`AutomaticCanvasSize` ignored the cards' aspect ratio and cut the row off after ~10 cards), and you can drag a row sideways with the mouse (`startDrag`; touch drags natively). A drag that moved more than 6 px doesn't count as a click on the card under it. Clicking a card sends `Equip` ("Character"/"Kart"/"Wheels", id); the server checks the id is in the Garage (or `Mascots.KINDS`, or `"Avatar"`) and sets the attribute. It's used from your next race. `GarageClient` preloads the Garage meshes (`ContentProvider:PreloadAsync`) before drawing: a ViewportFrame never shows a mesh that finished loading after it was put in.
- Picks are saved in the `Garage_v1` DataStore (`SETTINGS.GARAGE_DATASTORE`) as `{ Kart, Wheels, Character }`, loaded on join (bad ids are ignored), saved on leave and on server shutdown, only if changed (3 tries). Shutdown waits for every save still running (`garageSaving`), including the one started when the last player left; otherwise the server closed mid-save. DataStores only work in the published place with "Enable Studio Access to API Services" on, so test saving there, not from the `.rbxlx` file. A shop or locked items later would need an owned-items list there too, checked in the `Equip` handler.
- Adding a kart or wheel set: model it (see `docs/blender.md`), import it, put the template in the folder with the right centre and orientation, mark the paintable parts with a `Paint` attribute, then add a line to `KARTS`/`WHEELS`.

## Characters
- The `Mascots` ModuleScript (ReplicatedStorage, so the garage menu can preview them) holds the 18 characters as simple shape lists (rounded "Egg" parts are Parts with a Sphere `SpecialMesh`), plus `Mascots.build`, which makes drivers, grandstand fans, statues and the garage previews. A driver sits at `Mascots.SEAT` from the kart's hitbox. An entry's `name` is what the menu shows (default: its id). `headAt` moves the head (default 3 studs above the seat); `ball = true` leaves out the body and arms, so the character is just its head.
- Playable characters: in the garage's CHARACTER row a player picks "My Avatar" (`Character` = `"Avatar"`, their own Roblox avatar, shown by its headshot) or any of the 18. In a race, `addRacer` hides their avatar (every part and decal goes to Transparency 1) and welds the character into the kart the same way as a CPU's. Respawning in the lobby gives them their normal avatar back. Characters only show in the kart: they're built sitting down with no legs, so in the lobby you walk around as your avatar. Players can pick the same character as each other or as a CPU. CPUs never share one: each CPU name has its own character in `BOTS.MASCOTS`, and names never repeat in a race. Keep new characters friendly and original: the frog and cat were redesigned once because they looked too much like a meme and a famous brand.
- The one deliberate exception is **Feelsgood Fred** (`Fred`): the owner asked for a recognisable homage to the famous meme frog (big droopy-lidded eyes, wide lips, blue shirt), knowing the small copyright risk. Never use the original character's name anywhere in the game's code or text. He's a CPU racer ("Fred (CPU)"), cheers in Croaker Swamp's stands, sits crowned on top of its temple (`buildFred`) and on its billboard (`buildFredBillboard`, `FRED_BILLBOARD`).
- The second exception is **Grand Dad** (`GrandDad`), the hero of the 1992 "7 Grand Dad" bootleg, asked for by the owner from its title-screen sprite (`assets/7granddad.png`). He keeps the sprite's own colours (rust cap and shirt, white face and gloves, blue moustache and hair, bright orange overalls) and has a "7" badge on his cap. Keep him looking like the bootleg, not the plumber it copied: no letter on the cap, no red/blue colours, and never that character's name in code or text. He's a CPU racer ("Grand Dad (CPU)").
- The third is **Smiley** (`Smiley`), asked for by the owner from `assets/verity.png` (a character from a 2026 Minecraft ARG): just a big yellow ball (`ball = true`, head 3.4 across) with a face on the front: black oval eyes in a soft dark-gold glow, and a wide crescent grin of white teeth with curled corners. The face pieces lie flat on the ball's surface (`onBall` and `smileyFace` in Mascots); a grin made of overlapping eggs bulged out in lumps. In the game he's only ever called Smiley. He's a CPU racer ("Smiley (CPU)").
- The fourth is **Mr. Tele Vision** (`TeleVision`), asked for by the owner from `assets/tenna.png` ("similar but a little different"): a showman with a TV for a head (a `Block` head). Made his own: a mint-green retro set with two gold dials on its right side and a dark screen with a glowing cyan face (two eyes, a wide grin), a V of antennae with red ball tips, a royal-blue suit with a white shirt and gold bow tie, white gloves. Keep him different from the original (not a lavender TV, no red suit or yellow necktie). He's a CPU racer ("Mr. Tele Vision (CPU)").
- Grandstand fans are the theme's characters (`Crowd` in `THEMES`) in random team colours, arms up. Their parts are anchored; the fan model's `Cheer` attribute sets when it hops. The client makes them hop with `workspace:BulkMoveTo`, only when the camera is within 250 studs.

## Physics and bumping
- Kart physics: the kart hovers at its `RideHeight` attribute on raycast springs (`senseGround` / `driveStep`, run in `RunService.PreSimulation`); the hitbox only ever touches walls. Wall hits are detected from unexpected velocity changes and deflect the kart. On off-road the top speed drops to `OFFROAD_SPEED` (unless boosting) and dust in the ground's colour flies off the wheels. You can drift on off-road (from a lower speed, `DRIFT_MIN_SPEED` × `OFFROAD_SPEED`), but it charges no mini-turbo, and any charge is lost there; otherwise drifting through mud would cancel its slow-down.
- Bumping is on for every track and is meant to be chaotic: a big shove, a hop, a random twist and a moment of slippery tyres (`BUMP_*` in the client's `HANDLING`; bots use the same values in `BOTS`). A track's `BUMPING` (default 1) becomes its `Bumping` attribute and scales the shove. Karts still pass through each other physically (collision groups), because client-owned karts colliding is laggy. Instead, each client shoves only its own kart away from nearby karts (`bumpKarts`), using where it sees them on screen (there used to be a `BUMP_LEAD` that looked ahead of other karts to make up for lag; it made their bump zone stick out in front of them, so it's gone). On ice the shove slides a long way.
- Bumping CPUs: bots are moved by the server, which sees a player's kart a moment late (and the player sees the bot a moment late), so the server alone hardly ever saw a player touch a bot. When a player's client bumps a bot it sends `Bump` (the bot's kart). The server checks the two karts are within `BOTS.BUMP_REPORT_RANGE` (40) of each other and sets `ai.hitBy`, and the bot's next step counts it as a bump and works out the shove itself (same cooldown as normal bumps).
- Who hit whom: each side compares how fast it and the other kart were heading at each other (`blame`, 1 = I drove into them, -1 = they drove into me, 0 = head-on). The rammer's shove and twist are scaled down towards `BUMP_RAMMER` (0.35), the kart that got hit gets up to `BUMP_RAMMED` (1.3, applied after the `BUMP_MAX` cap). Without this, rear-ending someone threw you back hard (a backward shove kills your speed, which then has to build up again) while the kart in front barely moved (a forward shove above top speed fades within a second).

## Controls
- Controls while driving: W/S gas and brake, A/D steer, Space drift, E use item (or click the item icon), Q look back (hold), R respawn. Gamepad: RB drift, X item, LB look back, Y respawn. On touch screens the actions are bound without Roblox's own buttons; instead `touchPad` (bottom right, sized from the screen height) has a big DRIFT button, BACK beside it and a see-through red RESET above, and you tap the item icon to use an item. Each button follows the finger that pressed it (`touchButton`), so sliding off it still lets go. Touch screens skip the controls hint at the start of a race.
- The race HUD: place and coins top left with the item icon under them, and a "Time left" bar top centre that's only as wide as its text (smaller on phones). There's no race timer counting up any more.
