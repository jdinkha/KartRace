# Track design

Read before designing a new track or changing a track's layout, areas, shortcuts or hazards.

The goal is for each track to feel like a level in a video game (think of driving through a 3D Zelda dungeon or a Mario Kart world). It should not feel like a real-world circuit (NASCAR/F1: one line of constant width with a few turns). It's still a race from the start line to one finish line, but the trip goes through distinct places.
- **Theme.** Each level should have a "theme". For example, Frosty Peaks has a "Snow" theme. This is important for the background music especially, because each "theme" will have its own song eventually.
- **A string of named areas.** Split each track into about 4–6 areas. Each gets its own name (on a `Banner`), its own look and its own challenge. Change the pace between them: open arena → tight cliff road → cave → big jump.
- **Rooms joined by corridors.** Like a dungeon: wide `Room` sections (arenas, plazas, caverns, 60–140 studs wide) connected by normal roads and `Tunnel` caves.
- **Using walled, linear roads.** Do not feel inclined to make numerous straighforward, walled off roads (straight or nearly straight lines with walls on the left and right). Instead, prefer the use of elevation, unwalled roads, bendy turns, obstacles, open terrains, etc.
- **Reference:** In 'assets/img1.png', you can see that the overall design of the level does not have to be linear, it can have branching paths. In 'assets/img2.png', you can see that the off-road should serve as an obstacle that racers must drive around, but also serve as potential shortcuts if players can boost or jump over it. In both images, you can see interesting world-building (mountains, trees, waterfalls, Yoshi eggs, a Yoshi billboard with Yoshis watching us on the cliffs), take inspiration from that.
- **Choices and creative shortcuts.** Each track needs at least two shortcuts, and they should be inventive, not just "a narrower road". Mark each one with a `Banner`. Examples already in the game:
  - a hole you can drop through onto road below (Magma's Lava River Jump: the main road loops down and passes under its own jump, `Skippable`)
  - a secret cave behind a waterfall (Jungle)
  - a boost-only leap across a ravine (Jungle)
  - an icy rail-less slide (Frosty)
  - a grass cut that's only fast if you hit the boost pad first (Skyway)
  - a risky geyser tube (Magma)
  - a windy, rail-less cliff ledge with one sharp 90° corner and a boost pad right before it as a trap: boost into the corner and you fly off (Frosty's Windy Ledge)

  A good shortcut saves a few seconds but costs something: risk of falling, slow off-road, hazards, or needing a boost.
- **Long branching paths (the Yoshi Valley rule).** A fork isn't a short detour beside the main road. In Yoshi Valley the paths split and don't meet again for 20–30 seconds; each one feels like its own little level, with its own look, hazards and rewards. Rules for every fork:
  - **Keep paths apart for a long time.** A branch should run on its own for at least ~15–20 seconds of driving (~1200–1600 studs at full speed), out of sight of the others where possible, and rejoin somewhere genuinely different from where it left (another room, halfway up a climb), not just a little further down the same room.
  - **Make every path a fair choice, with a different trade-off.** Each path should come out within a couple of seconds of the others for a good driver, by a different route: harder but shorter (narrow, no rails, hazards, a tough corner), or longer but with more coins, item boxes and boost pads, or safe and plain. Work the times out (length ÷ speed, minus boosts, plus off-road and hazard losses) and write them down next to the route.
  - **Never leave an obviously worse path.** If one path is clearly slower with nothing to show for it, nobody takes it. Moonlight Manor's Wine Racks was this: it split off to the side and rejoined a bit further on in the same cellar, while the main road went straight ahead, so there was no reason to take it. Now it continues on its own through the Wine Store at the back of the cellar and rejoins on a landing halfway up the Cellar Stairs at a tough 90° turn, a penalty that pays for its coins, item boxes and boost pads.
  - **Joining partway up a climb:** give the climb a flat landing and join there (a branch end must sit in a Room on flat road). Joining sideways onto a slope tilts the branch's end and makes the stairs' supports poke through it.
  - **Rewards on a branch:** branches only get one coin line by themselves, and item boxes only go on the main road. Add `Coins` and `Items` features to the branch.
  - **Where paths meet, make it an event**: a sharp turn, a jump down, a merge onto a climb. Not a quiet widening of the road.
- **Mega Jump sequence breaks are good.** A Mega Jump (about 45 studs up) over a wall or onto a ledge to skip part of the course takes skill, quick thinking and timing, and it's cool. Design for it: anywhere a jump could land must be solid (see CLAUDE.md), and the ground off the road there should let you get back to the road without a big penalty (Moonlight Manor's floors are only a little slower than the road, `floorSpeed` 0.85). The checkpoints allow it: passing any gate ahead counts, and the finish line always finishes you.
- **Off-road instead of bare walls.** Roads have slow off-road strips (grass, ash, snow, mud) between the road and the walls (`OFFROAD`); the walls sit at their outer edge. Boosting ignores off-road, so a boost pad before an off-road cut makes it a real shortcut. Make the non-drivable parts feel like you're driving in a real world rather than driving on a NASCAR track.
- **Race length.** A race should take about 1.5–2 minutes: around 7000–8500 studs of main road, with several distinct areas.
- **Hazards that fit the theme, with patterns players can learn.** Timed things warn before they fire (geysers bubble first). Moving things move steadily (fire bars, snowballs). Always leave a clear line through, and never place an unavoidable hit.
- **Height and landmarks.** Climb and drop, cross over or under other parts of the track, and give players something big to head for (the volcano, the sky rink).
- **Kid-friendly punishment.** Hazards slow you, spin you out, or send you back a little ("TOO HOT!", "WHOOPS!"). Nothing violent, but something a little mature, like a haunted house, is OK.
- **Room for a dozen karts.** Races have up to 12 racers (`MAX_RACERS`). The main road is never narrower than `ROAD_WIDTH` (46, about eight karts side by side). Only branches can be narrower (~26–32), because only part of the pack takes them.


**The one exception is Rainbow Skyway: the "vanilla" track** (like Mario Circuit). It's deliberately plain: wide (54), gentle and nearly straight, with walls everywhere and no hazards. Its extras stay simple: grass verges, a few cloud pillars to weave between, boost pads and one grass cut. It should still be a pretty track. New tracks should follow the level style above, not copy Skyway.

## Current tracks
Current tracks (all in the `RaceServer.Tracks` ModuleScript; Moonlight Manor has its own module inside it, `Tracks.Mansion`). Players pick one from the RACE menu (or, with `LOBBY_MODE` "Vote", vote between 3 at random, `VOTE_CHOICES`):
- **Rainbow Skyway** (Sky, vanilla, ~8600 studs): Into the Clouds (sweeping climb under a giant rainbow) → boost straight → Sunrise Curve → Cloud Bridge → Starlight Bend/Straight (cloud pillars) → Meadow Corner (Meadow Cut grass shortcut) → Sunset Sweep → Home Stretch. The grid is four karts wide.
- **Magma Mountain** (Lava, ~8400): Ember Fields → fork: Cinder Ridge or Lava Tube shortcut → Crater Hall inside the hollow volcano → Magma Depths → Sulfur Flats → Obsidian Bridges (no rails, fire bar) → Basalt Forest → Ashfall Canyon → Lava River Jump. Drop through the jump's hole to land on the Lava Falls Loop below, skipping most of it. The jump is `noReturn`: miss the road below (or the jump) and you're put back after the gap, so you go the long way round, and a low wall on the landing edge stops anyone driving back to drop in. High risk, high reward. Then Fire Temple → Temple Gate finish.
- **Frosty Peaks** (Snow, ~7070): Avalanche Pass → Pass Gate fork: Crystal Cave (a long winding cavern) or Windy Ledge (strong gusts, then a sharp 90° left with a trap boost pad in the middle of the road before it; the corner itself isn't icy) → Sky Rink → Frozen Bridge → Ski Jump → Snowy Lodge → Forest Clearing fork: Pine Forest or Ice Slide (icy, rail-less, a snowman right in the middle) → Snowman Village → Glacier Canyon → Frostbite Hills → finish.
- **Jungle Ruins** (Jungle, ~6800): River Crossing (rope bridges) → Boulder Steps → The Waterfall fork: Cliff Road or the Secret Cave hidden behind the waterfall → Temple of the Sun (a hall inside the hollow pyramid, water geysers) → Temple Stairs (boulders) → The Ravine: long windy rope bridge (`push` 60), or the Ravine Leap (needs a boost) → Mud Flats (all mud, with a line of boost pads) → Vine Tunnel → Crocodile Creek → finish.
- **Croaker Swamp** (Swamp, ~8100): a misty swamp at sunset; the water sits just under the road (`FALL_DEPTH` 8), so falling off anywhere drops you in and you respawn. Lily Pad Landing (start, Feelsgood Fred's billboard) → The Bog (a mud stretch, points 10–13, shortened by a quarter from 9–13, with one long, wide boost pad, `time` 2.4 and `width` 38 (most of the road, centred), just before it, a pair of frogs before and after the pad, and more frogs out in the mud: a pair in the middle, a single and a pair near the end) or the Boardwalk shortcut (rail-less planks over the water, 6 pairs of frogs hopping at random moments, about one hop a second; it rejoins the main road in a Room at points 13–14, just past the mud, so it skips only the mud, not the run up to the temple) → Temple Courtyard fork: the great hall of the Frog King's Temple (S-bends, 8 frog idols lashing their 34-stud tongues at random moments, each about every 3 s), out a side door and round the moat, or the High Passage shortcut (no barriers anywhere and frogs all the way: climb the temple's front steps on one dead-straight, smooth climb (`Flat`, evenly spaced eased points), fall off and you land on the temple's steps (`walkable`), which you can drive round, through a small doorway into a passage high above the hall, out a small doorway at the back, boost and leap across the moat (the ramp starts just inside the back doorway, `angle` 10, `gap` 143: you land on the main road behind the temple only with the boost; checked by simulating the flight, 115 studs/s lands ~14 studs clear and 96, top speed with 20 coins and no boost, falls in); it has `respawns = false`, so falling off puts you back in the courtyard in front of the temple) → The Overgrowth (an 88-wide, barrier-less room packed with 56 trees in staggered rows 29 studs apart (cut from 70 to make it a bit less dense), added by `forest()` in the Tracks module, the outermost right on the edge, plus 7 frogs hopping along the gaps between rows; rows 8 and 9 are left out, making a tree-free clearing across the road halfway through with a `Respawn` point in it) → Mushroom Glade (a winding cave of glowing mushrooms) → Lily Pad Sprint (rail-less planks over open water, boost pads, frogs) → finish. Feelsgood Fred sits on top of the temple.
- **Moonlight Manor** (Mansion, ~8800, `Tracks.Mansion`): a giant spooky-but-friendly mansion at midnight, at puppy scale (1 m of house ≈ 30 studs: chairs are cliffs, the dining table is a bridge). It starts on a dock at the foot of the cliff next to the lobby and climbs the whole house, one floor per area; on every floor the road splits in a room into several routes (one of them splits again) that all meet before that floor's staircase, Yoshi Valley style:
  - **Cellar** (Y 0): Wine Cellar fork → main road through the Boiler Room (steam vents), the Coal Cellar and the Laundry Room (soapy ice, laundry baskets) to the Cellar Stairs, two flights with a flat landing halfway (Y 45) / **Heating Pipe** (a tunnel, rejoins in the Coal Cellar) / **Wine Racks**, a level of its own (~1400 studs vs the main road's ~1320 over the same stretch): between barrel racks (splits again: the **Mouse Hole**, a narrow tunnel), through the wall into the **Wine Store** (the cellar's quiet north half, racks on both sides), back south into the laundry and up a ramp of crates onto the stairs' landing, where a hard right turn up the second flight is the price for its 2 boost pads, 3 extra coin lines and its own row of item boxes.
  - **Ground floor** (90): Kitchen fork → main road **Under the Table** (table legs to weave through, pouncing cats) / **Tabletop** (up a chair, between the cups, boost and jump off the end) / **Countertop** (up a step stool; splits again: the **Stovetop**, round hot rings that send you back). They meet in the Grand Foyer → Grand Staircase (red carpet runner).
  - **Bedrooms** (180): Upstairs Hall fork → main road through the Playroom (toy blocks, a rolling marble, hopping toy bunnies) / **Master Bedroom**, across the giant bed (splits again: **Under the Bed**, a dusty tunnel). They meet on the Landing → Gym (treadmill boosts, a giant fan's wind) → Attic Stairs.
  - **Attic** (270): main road among trunks and dust sheets (hopping owls, a draught through the broken window) / **The Rafters** (no rails, splits again: the **Tightrope**). Up the hatch.
  - **Roof** (330): main road between the chimneys (wind) / **Skylights**. Down the porch roof and the **Leap of Faith** (boost pad, ramp, 120-stud gap down into the garden), then the gravel drive to the Front Gates finish.
  - The floors are solid carpet (`Walkable`), only a little slower than the road (`floorSpeed` 0.85): fall off a table, a bed or a rafter, or Mega Jump over a wall, and you land on the floor of that room and can drive back onto the road. All the furniture, stairs and tunnels are solid too (a Mega Jump can reach them), and you can drive on top of them (the main road has no walls in the kitchen/dining/foyer for that). Indoors the roads never lean into corners (`MAX_BANK = 0`). CPUs take the main road and finish in about 2:00–2:15.
